The Antarctic ozone hole is the area south of 40°S with total column ozone below 220 DU. It is severe thinning, not an empty opening in the atmosphere.

01 / WHAT BREAKS IT DOWN?

Chlorine and bromine released from ozone-depleting chemicals such as CFCs and halons destroy stratospheric ozone. Cold polar conditions and returning spring sunlight intensify the seasonal loss over Antarctica.

02 / WHY DOES IT MATTER?

The stratospheric ozone layer absorbs harmful UV-B radiation. Ozone loss allows more UV-B to reach the surface. Total column ozone is measured in Dobson Units; it is different from ground-level ozone pollution.

03 / CAN THE SHIELD RECOVER?

The Montreal Protocol restricts ozone-depleting substances. Protecting the layer includes preventing refrigerant leaks and arranging proper recovery and disposal of older cooling equipment. Seasonal variation remains, so one day’s ozone-hole area is not a recovery trend.

04 / HOW TO READ THIS GLOBE

The raised shell and its colors are explanatory, not a global ozone measurement map. The pink Antarctic cap has the same surface area as the dated NASA ozone-hole statistic, arranged as a circle around the South Pole; its position and boundary do not reproduce the actual ozone-hole shape. Shell height is exaggerated. Ozone depletion and greenhouse warming are distinct problems; the ozone hole is not the main cause of global warming.

      This globe makes current heat and water visible at 48 initial sampled cities, plus any country reference location you select through country search. Temperature colors show absolute air temperature at 2 m, not a departure from a historical average. A hot day or an individual downpour does not by itself establish a climate trend or attribute an event to climate change.

      Open-Meteo provides model-based current conditions, not live sensors. Weather is fetched every 15 minutes; values change when the model’s valid time advances. Precipitation includes rain, showers and snow water equivalent accumulated over the current model interval (shown per location). The small chart shows up to six recent hourly modeled temperatures in Heat mode and precipitation totals for the preceding hour in Water mode.

      Colored halos surround sampled locations only. Their footprint and falling water particles are artistic symbols, not measured storm boundaries or a continuous global weather map. Unsampled areas have no weather overlay. Missing values are omitted; old data is labeled when refresh fails.

      The ozone layer uses NASA Ozone Watch daily Antarctic statistics. It retains a dated, bundled observation when the live source is inaccessible. The 3D shell is explanatory and its circular cap represents an equivalent area, not a satellite map or the observed outline. Source dates and saved-data status appear in its report.

      The separate seismic layer retains the USGS past-day feed with a 60-second refresh. Earthquakes are not presented as evidence or consequences of climate change. Repeating seismic rings locate records; they do not track current seismic waves.
