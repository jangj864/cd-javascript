How Earth responds

Real observations become heat, frost, tears, shaking and tired eyes.

Heat & frostNASA GISTEMP

The latest monthly global land–ocean temperature anomaly, compared with the 1951–1980 average. Positive values warm the rim from yellow to red; negative values add a blue rim and frost. The strongest artistic stages are at ±1.5°C, and the mouth opens above +1°C.

The blue ocean stays visible. These are visual thresholds, not official alerts or a local frost forecast.

Head shakingUSGS

The earthquake count in the last hour is compared with the preceding hour. An increase makes the shakes wider, faster and longer; a doubling reaches the strongest stage. Bursts ease in and out.

This is a separate geological feed. Earthquakes are not presented as a consequence of climate change, and catalogue counts depend on detection and reporting.

Flowing tearsOpen-Meteo

Current precipitation is sampled at 36 locations, including oceans, weighted by latitude-band area and converted to mm/hour. This coarse estimate is not an official measured global mean.

More rain progresses from watery eyes to continuous streams and sobbing. Stream width, length, speed and opacity increase together.

Rising waterNASA

Global mean sea-level change since 1993 also contributes to tears. This is a long-term observation, not a live tide or wave. The retained observation is +115.8 ±4.0 mm, October 2026.

Heavy eyesCAMS / Open-Meteo

Use Air to choose a city. Its current US AQI and PM2.5 model estimates are shown with the city and model time. The eyes grow heavier above AQI 50 and reach their strongest artistic expression at 200.

This local reading does not replace the global climate observations. Failed or stale data adds no fatigue. Your location preference and up to six saved cities or globe points are kept in this browser. The Air panel shows the returned JSON, HTTP status, received time and the exact condition used to choose its expression.

Quakes are checked every minute, weather and air every 15 minutes, and climate sources daily. Each source keeps its own observation date. Retained climate values stay dated; missing live feeds stay unavailable.
