    Human-caused warming is changing the conditions we live in, including heat extremes and heavy precipitation. Read the IPCC’s assessment ↗

    This globe makes current heat and water visible at 48 sampled cities. Temperature colors show absolute air temperature at 2 m, not a departure from a historical average. A hot day or an individual downpour does not by itself establish a climate trend or attribute an event to climate change.

    Open-Meteo provides model-based current conditions, not live sensors. Weather is fetched every 15 minutes; values change when the model’s valid time advances. Precipitation includes rain, showers and snow water equivalent accumulated over the current model interval (shown per location). The small chart shows up to six recent hourly modeled temperatures in Heat mode and precipitation totals for the preceding hour in Water mode.

    Colored halos surround sampled locations only. Their footprint and falling water particles are artistic symbols, not measured storm boundaries or a continuous global weather map. Unsampled areas have no weather overlay. Missing values are omitted; old data is labeled when refresh fails.

    The separate seismic layer retains the USGS past-day feed with a 60-second refresh. Earthquakes are not presented as evidence or consequences of climate change. Repeating seismic rings locate records; they do not track current seismic waves.
