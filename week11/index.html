a face, not a forecast.

The face is an artistic translation of four real data sources. Heat, head shaking and tears can happen together. Each has a gentle, medium and intense stage. Click the speech bubble for another thought about the current conditions. The pupils and face gently follow your pointer in all directions.

a warming atmosphere + a voice
NASA GISTEMP’s latest monthly global land–ocean temperature anomaly, relative to 1951–1980. The blue globe keeps its ocean and land colours. A separate translucent atmosphere shifts from yellow through orange to red and grows thicker and more opaque as the anomaly rises toward +1.5°C. The mouth opens above +1°C. The warmest red atmosphere is reached at +1.5°C. These are visual thresholds, not official alert levels. NASA GISTEMP ↗
shaking its head
The USGS feed’s earthquake count in the last hour compared with the preceding hour. A rise triggers intermittent head shaking. Its speed, angle and duration grow continuously with the relative increase; a doubling or greater increase produces the strongest shake. Each burst eases in and out. Catalog counts depend on detection and reporting; earthquakes are not presented as a consequence of climate change. USGS ↗
tears
An area-weighted estimate of current precipitation from 36 evenly spaced Open-Meteo sample points, including ocean locations. Precipitation is converted to mm/hour from its model interval; this coarse estimate is not an official measured global mean. A higher rain estimate progresses from water gathering under the eyelids to two continuous streams following the cheeks, then sobbing. Stream width, length, opacity and flow speed increase together; the strongest tears narrow the eyes, widen the mouth and add a small sobbing movement. Eyebrows, pupils and the mouth ease together between expressions. Open-Meteo ↗
and rising seas
NASA’s global mean sea-level change since 1993 also adds to the tears. This is a long-term measurement, not a live tide or wave. The retained value is +115.8 ±4.0 mm, October 2026; greater rise produces more tears. NASA sea level ↗

Quakes refresh every minute, weather every 15 minutes, and climate sources are checked daily. Each source has its own observation date. Saved climate readings stay dated if the source cannot be reached; missing live feeds stay marked unavailable.

Sound is synthesized in the browser and starts only when you turn it on. Reduced-motion settings keep the expressions still. The softly illustrated continents and facial features are rendered in 3D. The temperature atmosphere is an artistic overlay, not a measured atmospheric layer. Three.js ↗
